# Preview tile streaming contract

`main.ts` now connects the preview camera to `streaming.ts`: moving the camera selects nearby refs, the scheduler loads up to two tiles concurrently, and the view keeps the selected tiles that fit explicit workload and memory caps. It remains a non-playable geographic preview; it does not provide movement, collision, or a physical-device frame-rate guarantee.

## Selection

`selectVisibleTiles` accepts manifest refs, the camera's conservative WGS84 bounds, a WGS84 focus position, and explicit triangle, draw-call, resident-byte, and tile-count caps. It handles a view or tile whose west longitude is greater than its east longitude by splitting that range at ±180°. If both the view and tile reach the same pole, their longitude intervals are treated as meeting at that point. Tile bounds intersection determines visibility; nearest-first order is a great-circle distance to each tile-bounds center, with stable ID/hash tie breaks.

The conservative default reserves each ref's declared triangles and draw calls twice: once for color rendering and once again for shadow work. Resident bytes default to the ref's raw bytes. The preview supplies an explicit merged-renderer model: twice the manifest triangles, three calls, and `8 * ref.bytes + 192 * ref.triangles` estimated bytes per tile, with zero extra shadow overhead because that model already includes it. The preview caps a plan at eight tiles, 90,000 triangles and 40 calls after fixed ground/grid reserves, and 50 MB estimated resident bytes. The selector reports selected refs, visible refs deferred by a cumulative cap, individually overbudget refs, and refs outside the view separately. It does not skip a later small tile just because an earlier visible tile did not fit.

`view-bounds.ts` bounds the flat-ground footprint using all four projected camera-corner rays, including oblique far corners, then converts its radius around the target into a spherical great-circle bounding rectangle. A ray at or above the horizon explicitly exceeds the preview radius limit. The rectangle wraps across the antimeridian and expands to all longitudes if its disk reaches a pole. The camera footprint is explicitly capped at 5 km; the UI reports this limit. District buttons and previous/next controls focus the camera from tile bounds. Async tile arrivals never move the camera.

## Scheduler

`TileStreamScheduler` accepts one pinned manifest identity and a selection result. Its injected loader receives the ref, manifest identity, region ID, allowed source IDs, and abort signal. The loader must fetch, hash-check, and validate the payload before resolving, then echo the pinned identity and ref hash. The scheduler checks these echoed identities, allows at most `WORLD_LIMITS.maxFetches` requests at once, aborts requests when the selection or manifest changes, and publishes only results that are still selected for the current manifest.

Validated payloads are cached by `manifest identity + tile SHA-256` in the existing `ByteLru`, capped at 50 MB estimated payload storage and 512 entries. The preview charges eight times raw UTF-8 bytes for parsed payload storage; this is an estimate, not a measured browser heap ceiling. One scheduler persists across manifest changes, so aborted old requests retain slots until settled and all manifest-scoped caches share the same limit. Active renderer values are created by an injected `activate` callback and removed through `disposeActive` when selection changes, the manifest changes, or the scheduler is disposed. A failed tile stays failed while it remains selected; camera selection updates do not restart it. The visible Retry failed tiles button is the explicit retry path. Settled notifications remove completed requests from `pendingIds` before publishing the snapshot. The UI separately displays loaded, pending, deferred, overbudget, failed, and outside-view counts and names deferred/failed refs.

## Limits before integration

The preview pins one ENU origin at the manifest region bounds center (height zero) for the manifest lifetime and projects every active tile directly into it. Before adding each tile's meshes, the renderer measures merged triangle counts, shadow work, calls, and an active-memory estimate from parsed payload size and CPU/GPU typed-array copies. Over-cap geometry is disposed before it is published, and already active tiles remain visible. The payload cache is separate from this active-memory estimate.

Tests use synthetic refs and injected loaders only. They cover dateline and polar selection, stable priority, cumulative and per-tile budget outcomes, renderer cost overrides, concurrency, aborts, stale results, manifest isolation, disposal, cache entry/byte limits, and camera-circle bounds at the antimeridian and poles. Browser QA verified Accra focus changes, cache reuse on return, idle demand rendering, and switching to London at the actual 586×804 viewport. Accra drew 8 active tiles with 5,530 color-pass triangles / 18 calls; its far focus drew 7,186 / 17. London drew 8 tiles with 11,854 / 17. Conservative shadow-inclusive admission counts remain separate from the color-pass readout. No new console errors occurred after the final reload; earlier intermediate HMR errors were resolved. Additional viewport, rendering-budget rejection, long-running eviction/disposal and physical-device checks remain acceptance gates.
